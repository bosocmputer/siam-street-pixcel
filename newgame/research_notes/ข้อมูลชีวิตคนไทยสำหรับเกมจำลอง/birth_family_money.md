# Starting conditions of a person born in Thailand today: population, family, household money (for a Thai life-sim game)

Research date: 22 Oct 2026 (2569). Method note: direct page fetching was blocked in this environment (the proxy refused connections to nso.go.th, matichon.co.th, naewna.com, etc.), so every figure below comes from search-engine extractions of the cited pages, not from reading the PDFs/tables myself. Figures that appeared in only one source, or that disagreed between sources, are flagged. Buddhist-era years: 2568 = 2025, 2569 = 2026.

## 1. Where a baby is born: registered population, regions, births, urban/rural

### Takeaway
Registered population at 31 Dec 2025 was 65,809,011 (falling for several years). Only about 416,600 babies were registered as born in 2025, the lowest in 75 years. Bangkok alone accounts for about 11% of births. A full province table exists on the DOPA site, but regional totals were not found pre-summed.

### Cited Findings
- Registered population (ราษฎรทั่วราชอาณาจักร) at 31 Dec 2568 (2025): **65,809,011**. Of these, **64,820,708 are Thai nationals and 988,620 are not Thai nationals**. Announced by the Central Registration Office (สำนักทะเบียนกลาง) on 25 Mar 2569 (2026) — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/quality-life/news_5649652); [Naewna](https://www.naewna.com/local/954864); [The Coverage](https://www.thecoverage.info/news/content/10963)
- The 2024 (2567) registered total was 65,951,210, so the population fell by about 142,199 in 2025 — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/quality-life/news_5649652)
- Conflict: Prachachat reports 997,549 non-Thai nationals and Bangkok at 5,455,020, which differs from other reports of the same announcement — [Prachachat](https://www.prachachat.net/hilight-prachachat/news-1984243)
- Largest provinces in 2025 (registered): Bangkok 5,422,568; Nakhon Ratchasima 2,613,132; Ubon Ratchathani 1,865,501; Chiang Mai 1,798,588; Khon Kaen 1,785,462. Half of the top 10 provinces are in the Northeast (Isan) — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/quality-life/news_5649652); [Postjung summary](https://board.postjung.com/1682654)
- Province, district, subdistrict and village tables are published on the DOPA statistics site (stat.bora.dopa.go.th). Regional totals have to be summed from the province rows — [DOPA stat](https://stat.bora.dopa.go.th/new_stat/webPage/statByYear.php)
- Registered vs actual residence: the registry counts people by house registration (ทะเบียนบ้าน), not where they live. Many people move to Bangkok and other cities for work or study without moving their registration — [search summary of Prachachat/DOPA coverage](https://www.prachachat.net/general/news-1984243)
- UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 puts the **Bangkok urban agglomeration at about 18.18 million** in 2025, compared with 5.42 million registered in Bangkok province — [Worldometer (UN WUP)](https://www.worldometers.info/it/popolazione/citta-piu-grandi-in-thailandia/)
- Births registered in 2025: **416,574**, the lowest in 75 years. Deaths were **559,684**. Deaths have exceeded births for 5 years in a row. 2024 was the first year with fewer than 500,000 births — [The Standard](https://thestandard.co/thailand-birth-crisis-solutions/); [Bangkok Biznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/health/public-health/1215717)
- Births by province in 2025: Bangkok ranked 1st with **45,685** births. Chonburi ranked 2nd, with 18,061 per Bangkok Biznews but 19,939 per Prachachat (Prachachat's number may be copied from 2024). Narathiwat is listed with 9,376 births by Prachachat but is not in Bangkok Biznews's list (unconfirmed). The fewest births were in Samut Songkhram (598) and Chai Nat (986) — [Bangkok Biznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/health/public-health/1215717); [Prachachat](https://www.prachachat.net/hilight-prachachat/news-1984243); [Thaiger](https://thethaiger.com/th/news/1584598/)
- January to June 2026: **176,445 births**, lower than the same period in 2025 — [The Prachakorn / IPSR Mahidol](https://www.theprachakorn.com/newsDetail.php?id=1220)
- Urban share (UN definition): **52.8% (2024), 53.5% (2025), 54.3% (2026)**, up from 43.0% in 2010. This is a UN modelled series, not an NSO municipal-area figure — [Worldometer (UN data)](https://srv1.worldometers.info/world-population/thailand-population/)

### Inferences
- Birth-place weighting for the game: Bangkok ≈ 45,685 / 416,574 ≈ **11.0% of births**, above its 8.2% share of the registered population (5.42M / 65.81M). Bangkok has younger in-migrants and births are registered where they happen (calculated from the cited figures).
- Non-Thai-national registered residents ≈ 988,620 / 65.8M ≈ **1.5%** of the registered population. The real share of non-citizens is higher, because unregistered migrants are not counted (see section 2).
- If Narathiwat's 9,376 births (≈2.3% of national births) is correct, the Deep South has much higher fertility than its population share (Narathiwat ≈ 0.8M people). This is plausible but unconfirmed.
- For a regional spawn table, use the DOPA province CSV and sum by region. Isan holds the most people, with 5 of the top 10 provinces.

### Gaps
- Regional population and birth totals for 2025 were not found pre-summed. The DOPA province table must be downloaded and aggregated (blocked here).
- No NSO municipal/non-municipal (ในเขต/นอกเขตเทศบาล) urban share for 2025 was found. The UN figure uses a different definition.
- No official estimate of Bangkok's unregistered (de facto) population was found, beyond the UN agglomeration figure, which covers a wider area than Bangkok province.

## 2. Who the baby is: sex ratio, religion, ethnicity/language, stateless and migrant children

### Takeaway
Thailand's sex ratio at birth is about 106 boys per 100 girls. About 93% of people are Buddhist and 5% Muslim (Muslims are about 80% in Pattani and Narathiwat). About a third of Thais speak Isan/Lao as a first language. Around 1 million registered residents are non-Thai nationals, and several hundred thousand people are stateless or have unresolved status.

### Cited Findings
- Sex ratio at birth: the UN WPP 2024 puts it at about **106.4 boys per 100 girls**, historically between 106.3 and 106.5 — [Statistics Times](https://statisticstimes.com/demographics/country/thailand-sex-ratio.php)
- NSO Statistical Yearbook 2025: 2023 registered births were 256,663 boys and 242,325 girls, which works out to ≈ 105.9 (the search tool calculated this from the yearbook counts) — [NSO Statistical Yearbook 2025](https://www.nso.go.th/public/e-book/Statistical-Yearbook/SYB-2025/87/)
- Religion (official 2018 figures as cited by Wikipedia): **Buddhism 93.46%, Islam 5.37%, Christianity 1.13%, other 0.03%** — [Wikipedia: Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand)
- Non-census 2023 estimates differ: Buddhists about 90%, Muslims 4–5%, Christians "about 4%". These are treated as weak estimates — [Wikipedia: Religion in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Thailand)
- Deep South: Islam is the majority religion in Narathiwat, Yala, Satun and Pattani. Narathiwat is about **82% Muslim and 17.9% Buddhist** (year not stated). Narathiwat and Pattani were both over 80% Muslim in the 2000 census — [Wikipedia: Narathiwat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narathiwat_province); [US State Dept IRF 2010](https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/usdos/2010/en/22077)
- A province-by-province table of religion and language exists on Wikipedia (sourced from the census) — [Wikipedia: Nationality, religion, and language data for the provinces of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality,_religion,_and_language_data_for_the_provinces_of_Thailand)
- Language shares (Minority Rights Group): **Central Thai 34.1%, Isan/Thai-Lao 24.9%, Northern Thai (Kham Mueang) 9.9%, Southern Thai (Pak Tai) 7.5%** — [Minority Rights Group](https://minorityrights.org/country/thailand/)
- Wikipedia's first-language shares are higher: about **40% Central Thai, 33% Isan, 11% Northern Thai, 9% Southern Thai** — [Wikipedia: Languages of Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Thailand)
- Isan speakers are about a third of Thailand's population, roughly 13–16 million native speakers (2005 estimate) — [Wikipedia: Isan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan_language)
- Thai-Chinese: about **12–15%** of the population (MRG: 9–10 million) — [Wikipedia: Thai people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_people); [Minority Rights Group](https://minorityrights.org/country/thailand/)
- Patani Malays: about **1.5 million (2–2.5%)**. Kuy/Kuay/Suai and other Khmer/Austroasiatic groups: about 1.3–1.5 million — [Minority Rights Group](https://minorityrights.org/country/thailand/); [Wikipedia: Patani Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patani_Malays)
- Thailand has some 70 ethnic groups, including 11 Sino-Tibetan "hill tribe" groups. Karen is the largest — [Wikipedia: Ethnic groups in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Thailand)
- Minorities and ethnic groups holding registration records (ทะเบียนประวัติ) but not Thai nationality fell from **495,186 (June 2025) to 383,959 (June 2026)**. The drop followed the Cabinet resolution of 29 Oct 2024, in effect from 28 Jun 2025, which sped up nationality approval (from up to 270 days to 5 days) — [Lanner News](https://lannernews.com/24072569-01/); [Hfocus](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2025/06/34551)
- Chiang Mai alone has about **97,000 people** targeted by that Cabinet resolution — [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/354386)
- An academic paper cites **539,696 people who are without nationality but not stateless** (ไร้สัญชาติแต่ไม่ไร้รัฐ), including 36,943 children of minorities. The data year is unclear — [TCI journal (UBU)](https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jla_ubu/article/download/287954/189198/1276947)
- Children of non-Thai parents born in Thailand (cumulative DOPA records, partly illegible scan): about 110,516 children of minority groups (ID numbers starting 0 or 7) and about 131,768 children of migrant workers (ID numbers starting 00). Birth certificates for these children use form ท.ร.031 — [DOPA status document](https://www.bora.dopa.go.th/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Status-of-person.pdf); [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/thailand/media/5861/file)
- In January 2025, more than 110,000 children born in Thailand to foreign parents applied for Thai nationality — [MGR Online](https://mgronline.com/specialscoop/detail/9680000000513)
- Some migrant parents pay brokers to get a birth certificate because employers' paperwork is hard to obtain — [Thairath Plus](https://plus.thairath.co.th/topic/politics&society/104334)
- Since 2008, Thai law requires every child born in Thailand to have their birth registered, whatever the parents' status — [Hfocus](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2018/09/16265)

### Inferences
- A defensible weighted "language at home" table for a newborn: Central Thai ~35–40%, Isan/Lao ~25–33%, Northern ~10%, Southern ~8–9%, Malay (Yawi) ~2–3%, Khmer/Kuy ~2%, hill-tribe languages ~1–2%, other/migrant languages ~1–3%. Chinese-Thai is mostly an ancestry layer on top of Central Thai, not a separate home language. This is my estimate, combining the cited ranges.
- Religion should be conditional on region. Outside the Deep South, ~95%+ Buddhist. In Pattani, Yala and Narathiwat, ~80% Muslim. Christians are concentrated among some hill tribes (e.g., Karen) and in some Northern and Bangkok communities (inference; no regional Christian figure found).
- A "born stateless / ท.ร.031 / migrant child" start is real and plausible at roughly 1–3% of births. Border provinces (Tak, Chiang Rai, Chiang Mai, Mae Hong Son, Ranong, Samut Sakhon) are the most likely places (estimate; no annual count found).

### Gaps
- No official 2024/2025 sex ratio at birth was found. No NSO 2020/2023 religion-by-province table was retrieved (fetch blocked).
- No annual number of births to migrant mothers (Myanmar/Cambodia/Lao) was found. No 2026 count of registered migrant workers by nationality was found (the search only returned renewal deadlines: Lao/Myanmar/Vietnam permits valid to 11 Dec 2026, renewable to 11 Dec 2027, per [VoiceTV](https://www.voicetv.co.th/read/fK1og-WuR)).
- No reliable combined total for hill-tribe populations was found.

## 3. Household money: income, poverty, debt, inequality

### Takeaway
In 2025 the average household earned **28,308 baht a month** and spent 22,420. Average debt per household was 153,038 baht. Income fell 2.5% from 2023, the first fall in six years. Poverty dropped from about 4.9% (2024) to **3.27% (2025, 2.30 million people)**. The South, Northeast and North are the poorest regions, and Mae Hong Son and the Deep South are the poorest provinces.

### Cited Findings
- **NSO Socio-Economic Survey (SES) 2025 (2568), 57,600 households:** average household income **28,308 baht/month**, expenditure **22,420 baht/month**, average debt **153,038 baht per household** (reported as "5.4 times income", meaning monthly income). Income was down 2.5% from 2023, the first decline in 6 years, mainly due to lower income from work — [NSO SES 2568 report](https://www.nso.go.th/nsoweb/storage/survey_detail/2026/20260505141117_55523.pdf); [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/3272815/average-thai-household-income-declined-in-2025); [Nation / SCB EIC](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40067288)
- SES 2025, first 6 months only: income 28,151 baht and debt 144,871 baht. Do not mix these with the full-year numbers — [NSO](https://www.nso.go.th/nsoweb/storage/survey_detail/2025/20250924095708_31280.pdf)
- KResearch, analysing SES 2025: about **23.8% of indebted households** have monetary income below their expenses. Debt among low-income households (<15,000 baht/month) rose 1.9% — [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40067288)
- **SES 2023 (2566):** income 29,030 baht/month and expenditure 23,695 baht/month. Debt per household was 197,255 baht, up from 156,770 baht in 2015 — [Thairath](https://www.thairath.co.th/money/economics/thai_economics/2777868); [Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/business/economy/593290)
- SES 2023 by region:
  - Bangkok income **40,051 baht/month**, of which 30,631 was from work — [NSO Bangkok Statistical Data 2566](https://www.nso.go.th/public/e-book/Other-Publications/Bangkok_Statistical_Data_2566/32/)
  - Northeast income **22,524 baht/month**, the lowest region (cited by Isan Insight from NSO, not verified against the NSO table) — [Isan Insight (KKU)](https://isaninsight.kku.ac.th/archives/5708)
  - Regional expenditure: Central 24,635, South 23,263, North 18,961, Northeast 18,676 baht/month — [NSO Income-2566 provincial report](https://www.nso.go.th/public/e-book/Analytical-Reports/Income-2566/8/)
- **Poverty 2024 (2567, NESDC):**
  - **3.43 million poor, 4.89%** of the population, up from 3.41% in 2023. Some outlets say 3.41 million or 4.85%.
  - Poverty line **3,078 baht per person per month**.
  - Very poor (จนมาก): 879,000. Near-poor: 4.29 million (6.10%).
  - Poor households: 1.03 million (3.68% of households).
  - Agricultural workers made up 45.49% of the increase in the poor.

  — [Bangkok Biznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/business/economic/1199118); [Amarin TV](https://www.amarintv.com/spotlight/economy/525423); [LINE Today](https://today.line.me/th/v3/article/vXGB1r8)
- Poverty 2024 by region (single extraction, not verified): **South 9.43%, Northeast 6.56%, North 5.75%**. The Bangkok and Central figures were not found — [search summary of NESDC coverage via Naewna/Thai PBS](https://policywatch.thaipbs.or.th/article/government-211)
- Ten provinces with the highest poverty in 2024: **Mae Hong Son, Yala, Pattani, Narathiwat, Ubon Ratchathani, Sa Kaeo, Phatthalung, Sisaket, Chiang Rai, Tak**. Mae Hong Son and Pattani have been in the top 5 for at least 15 years — [Sanook](https://www.sanook.com/news/9867870/); [Posttoday](https://www.posttoday.com/smart-city/730744); [Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/economy/639421)
- **Poverty 2025 (2568, NESDC):** **2.30 million poor, 3.27%**, down from 3.41 million (4.85%). Near-poor: **3.6 million**. Government transfers to the poor rose by an average of 346 baht/person/month. NESDC credits La Niña (good harvests) and state support — [Naewna](https://www.naewna.com/n/like-learn/104883/)
- Gini (World Bank series): **0.335 (2023), 0.333 (2024)** — [CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/thailand/social-gini-coefficient-annual/gini-coefficient); [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SIPOVGINITHA)
- A 2025 paper citing NESDC: in 2023 the top 10% received **32.42%** of income and the bottom 50% received **22.49%**. This conflicts with the World Bank top-10% share of 26.0% (2024), probably because of different definitions — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23322039.2025.2461597?af=R); [TheGlobalEconomy](https://www.theglobaleconomy.com/Thailand/gini_inequality_index/)
- Household debt to GDP (Bank of Thailand):
  - Q1/2026: **85.9%**, the lowest in about 6 years
  - Q2/2025: 86.8%
  - Q2/2024: 16.32 trillion baht, 89.6% of GDP

  — [Bangkok Biznews editorial](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/blogs/opinion/editorial/1244115); [Thai PBS Policy Watch](https://policywatch.thaipbs.or.th/article/economy-285); [Thai Newsroom](https://thainewsroom.com/2026/10/12/thai-household-debt-my-ease-to-84-of-gdp-by-yearend/)
- A different survey (UTCC, small sample): average debt **740,000 baht per indebted household in 2025**, the highest in 4 years. It surveys indebted households only, so it is not comparable with NSO's all-household average — [UTCC](https://www.utcc.ac.th/utcc-household-debt-2568/)
- A 2026 survey found **62% of Thais in debt** (a separate survey of workers, not NSO) — [Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40065627)
- The BOT Northeast economic structure 2025 gives average family size **3.7 persons/household**, with 2.4 working-age (16–64) people per household — [BOT NE structure 2025](https://www.bot.or.th/content/dam/bot/documents/th/thai-economy/regional-economy/northeastern/economic-structure/2568/2025-ne-structure-v2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Poverty line in game terms: 3,078 baht/person/month → a family of 4 is poor below ~12,300 baht/month (calculated).
- The national average is 28,308 baht/month. Bangkok households (~40k) earn about 1.8× Isan households (~22.5k) (2023 data). Use a skewed (log-normal-like) distribution, because the mean is well above the median when Gini is ~0.33–0.43. I found no median figure.
- Suggested game "family wealth class" seed (my estimate, not a source table):
  - very poor 1–2%
  - poor 3–5% (higher in the South, Northeast, North and Deep South)
  - near-poor 5–6%
  - lower-middle ~40%
  - middle ~35%
  - upper-middle ~10%
  - rich ~2–3%

  Weight by province poverty: Mae Hong Son and Pattani births should draw poor far more often.
- Debt is the norm: a baby is likely born into a household carrying some debt (NSO average 153k baht; BOT debt/GDP ~86%). The share of NSO households with any debt was not found. The popular "~50%+" figure is from a news summary, not a table.

### Gaps
- SES 2025 income, debt and the share of households in debt by region, plus income by quintile/decile, are in the NSO PDF, which could not be opened. Re-check the NSO 2568 SES tables (income by region, Table 4/5) when fetching is possible.
- 2025 poverty by region was not found. The 2024 regional rates (South 9.43 / NE 6.56 / North 5.75) come from a single extraction.
- No household wealth/asset distribution (land, house, vehicle ownership) was found.
- No median household income was found.

## 4. Family structure: parents, grandparents, divorce, teen births, siblings

### Takeaway
About 1 in 4 Thai children (≈3 million) do not live with either parent, mostly because parents work elsewhere. About 71–79% of those children live with grandparents, rising to about 1 in 3 children in Isan. Fertility is about 1.0, so most newborns will be an only child or have one sibling. Divorces run at about 58% of the number of new marriages.

### Cited Findings
- **MICS 2022 (UNICEF/NSO):** about **25% of children under 18 (~3 million) do not live with either parent**, mostly because parents migrate for work. **71% of them live with grandparents** — [UNICEF Thailand press release](https://www.unicef.org/thailand/press-releases/national-survey-shows-progress-breastfeeding-adolescent-births-and-violence-worrying); [UNICEF on X](https://x.com/UNICEF_Thailand/status/1681273268643217416)
- Northeast: about **1 in 3 children** live apart from both biological parents (another UNICEF brief: **36%**; 79% of these with grandparents). Nationally the share rose from about 1 in 5 (2005) to 1 in 4 (2022) — [UNICEF Fulfilling the Commitment to Child Protection](https://data.unicef.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/UNICEF_Thailand_FNL-pages.pdf); [UNICEF Addressing the Gaps (MICS 2022)](https://www.unicef.org/thailand/media/12776/file/Addressing%20the%20Gap%20(MICS2022)%20EN.pdf.pdf)
- MICS 2015–16 (MICS5): about 1 in 5 children (>3.1 million) lived without either living biological parent, with the highest share in the Northeast — [MICS5 fact sheet](https://www.chonlatit.com/data/mch/MICS5FactSheet_TH.pdf)
- Skipped-generation households (grandparents + grandchildren only) were **about 15% of households in 2014** (weakly sourced; the original source is not named) — [The Prachakorn (IPSR)](https://www.theprachakorn.com/newsDetail.php?id=81); framework in [UNFPA State of Thai Population 2015](https://thailand.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/pub-pdf/State%20of%20Thailand%20Population%20report%202015-Thai%20Family_th.pdf)
- **TFR 2024 = 1.0** (IPSR Mahidol). The Department of Health gave 1.12 for 2023. One article claims 0.86 (unverified, likely a different method) — [IPSR Mahidol](https://ipsr.mahidol.ac.th/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/612-Thai-Pop-Situation-2569.pdf); [Matichon column](https://www.matichon.co.th/columnists/thinkstation-12/news_5872870)
- IPSR projects that if TFR falls to ~0.7, Thailand's population could fall to 61.9 million by 2035 (projection) — [IPSR press release Jan 2026](https://mahidol.ac.th/temp/2026/01/IPSR-MU-Press-Release-12-01-2569.pdf)
- **Divorces registered** (DOPA, via Prachachat): 110,942 (2021), 146,159 (2022), 147,337 (2023), 147,621 (2024), **139,100 (2025)** — [Prachachat](https://www.prachachat.net/hilight-prachachat/news-1964339)
- **Marriages registered:** about **240,979 (2025)** vs 263,087 (2024, a 10-year low). The IPSR wording is garbled, so this is moderately reliable. Same-sex marriages since marriage equality took effect: **25,814 couples** — [IPSR Mahidol](https://ipsr.mahidol.ac.th/en/post_multimedia/2025-02-01-btimes-divorce/); [Prachachat](https://www.prachachat.net/hilight-prachachat/news-1964339)
- Divorces by province, 2025 (from a forum post citing DOPA, unverified): Bangkok 15,118, Chonburi 7,039, Nakhon Ratchasima 5,157, Rayong 4,163, Samut Prakan 3,582 — [Pantip](https://pantip.com/topic/43930738)
- **Adolescent birth rate (15–19):**
  - **18.6 per 1,000 in 2024** (Department of Health). Target ≤15 by 2027; <0.5 per 1,000 for ages 10–14 — [Bangkok Biznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/health/public-health/1213048); [Hfocus](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2025/12/36486)
  - MICS: **18 per 1,000 in 2022**, down from 23 in 2019. Higher in the poorest quintile and among non-Thai populations — [MICS/UNICEF](https://mics.unicef.org/news/thailands-latest-mics-data-show-drop-adolescent-birth-rate-worrying-trend-nutrition-and-higher)
- Registered persons with disabilities: **2,261,154 (3.34% of the population) at 31 Oct 2025**, all ages (2,211,105 in Mar 2025). New disability criteria took effect 13 Dec 2025 — [Prachachat](https://www.prachachat.net/hilight-prachachat/news-1937439); [Senate](https://www.senate.go.th/)
- Older research counted **93,129 children with disabilities aged 0–14** (2019 study) and estimated that more than half of all disabled people are unregistered — [TCIJ](https://www.tcijthai.com/news/2019/07/scoop/8938)
- Child-care institutions: the North has the most state children's homes, linked to poverty. Baan Wiang Ping (Chiang Mai) places about 30–40 children a year for adoption. No national adoption count was found — [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/336762); [DCY](https://www.dcy.go.th/public/subwebsite/province/nakhonnayokshelter/file_download/1654769982769-828280308.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a newborn, "who raises you" weights could be:
  - national: both parents ~60–65%; grandparents with parents away ~18%; one parent ~12–15%; other relatives/institution ~3–5%
  - Isan: grandparent care ~28–30%

  These are my estimates. Only the 25% "neither parent" and 71–79% "with grandparents" figures are sourced; the one-parent share is not.
- Divorce/marriage ratio ≈ 139,100 / 240,979 ≈ **0.58** in 2025 (calculated). Many couples, especially rural ones, never register their marriage, so registered divorce understates separation.
- With TFR ~1.0 and about 417k births, a game should make "only child" or "one sibling" the common case. Large sibling sets fit best in the Deep South and among hill-tribe families (inference from the Narathiwat birth count and the higher teen-birth rates in poor and non-Thai groups).
- Teen-mother start: ~18 per 1,000 women aged 15–19. Births to under-20 mothers are probably ~5–8% of all births. This is a rough estimate, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No national single-parent household share (mother-only / father-only) was found. It is in the MICS 2022 full report table, which could not be opened: [MICS 2022 full report](https://www.unicef.org/thailand/media/11356/file/Thailand%20MICS%202022%20full%20report%20(English).pdf)
- No current (2023–2025) skipped-generation household share was found.
- No national counts were found for orphans, children in institutions, or adoptions per year. No 2025 count of children with disabilities aged 0–14 was found.

## 5. Parents' occupations

### Takeaway
About half of Thai workers are informal (no social security). Agriculture still employs a large share and is near 45% in poorer provinces, but it has shrunk for 7 straight quarters. A national 2025 sector breakdown was not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- Labour Force Survey Q2/2025: labour force **40.12 million**, employed **39.51 million**, unemployment **0.9%** — [NSO LFS Q2/2568](https://www.nso.go.th/nsoweb/storage/survey_detail/2025/20250808095045_97460.pdf)
- Q3/2025: about **39.9 million** employed (−0.5% y/y). Agricultural employment fell **2.9%**, its 7th straight quarterly fall, as young people leave farming — [NESDC Social Situation Q3/2568](https://www.nesdc.go.th/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/1.-publisher_-%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A0%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%B0%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A1%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%A2-%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A1-%E0%B8%9B%E0%B8%B5-2568.pdf)
- Informal workers (no social protection from work): **52.7% in 2024, ~52.4% in 2025** (≈20.9 million of 39.9 million) — [NESDC Q3/2568](https://www.nesdc.go.th/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/1.-publisher_-%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A2%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%99%E0%B8%A0%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%B0%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A1%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%A2-%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A1-%E0%B8%9B%E0%B8%B5-2568.pdf); [NSO informal employment survey](https://www.nso.go.th/nsoweb/storage/survey_detail/2026/20251126091907_74347.pdf)
- Province examples, 2025: **Satun 45.9%** of workers in agriculture (70,565 of 153,790). **Phayao 45.1%** in agriculture, forestry and fishing, its largest sector — [NSO Satun](https://satun.nso.go.th/); [NSO Phayao LFS FY2025](https://phayao.nso.go.th/images/ebook/TheLaborForceSurveyFiscal%20year%202025.pdf)
- Agricultural workers made up 45.49% of the increase in poor people in 2024 — [Amarin TV](https://www.amarintv.com/spotlight/economy/525423)

### Inferences
- Parent-occupation table for the game (my estimate, consistent with the cited informal ~52% and agriculture ~30% nationally / ~45% in rural provinces):
  - farmer/fisher 25–30% (rural Isan/North 40–50%; Bangkok <2%)
  - factory/private employee 20–25%
  - trader/small shop/street vendor/own-account 15–20%
  - service and gig work (rider, construction day labour) ~10%
  - civil servant/state enterprise/teacher/police/soldier ~7–8%
  - business owner/employer ~2–3%
  - unemployed or not working ~2–5%

  Roughly half of parents should have no social security (informal).
- The "farmer" share is shrinking and ageing. A newborn's grandparents are more likely farmers than its parents.

### Gaps
- No national 2025 employment-by-industry or by-work-status table (employer / government / private employee / own-account / unpaid family worker) was retrieved. This is the NSO LFS annual table; the PDF was blocked.
- No SES "socio-economic class of household" distribution (farm operators, workers, professionals, etc.) for 2023/2025 was found.

## 6. Welfare and rights at birth

### Takeaway
A Thai-national baby whose household earns under 100,000 baht/person/year currently gets **600 baht/month until age 6**, received by about 2.05–2.11 million children in 2026. The grant has been approved to become universal and start in pregnancy from FY2027 (Oct 2026). Health care is free under the 30-baht/UC scheme (≈99.6% coverage). Every birth must be registered, including children of migrants (form ท.ร.031).

### Cited Findings
- Child Support Grant (เงินอุดหนุนเพื่อการเลี้ยงดูเด็กแรกเกิด):
  - **600 baht/child/month**, paid on the 10th of each month
  - The October 2026 payment (9 Oct 2569) went to **2,052,557 children**; June 2026 to **2,109,498** — [Thairath Oct 2569](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/society/2965083); [Thairath Jun 2569](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/society/2938404)
  - Current eligibility: the child is a Thai national in a Thai house register, aged ≤6, not in a state institution, and household members' average income is **≤100,000 baht/person/year** — [Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/general-news/664050); [PPTV](https://www.pptvhd36.com/news/%E0%B8%AA%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%87%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A1/264333)
  - Registration happens where the family actually lives, not where the house register is: district offices in Bangkok, Pattaya city hall, and local administrations (อบต./เทศบาล) elsewhere — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/news_5754782)
  - History: the 2016 Cabinet extended it to ages 0–3 at 600 baht. It was later extended to age 6 — [MSO Khon Kaen](https://khonkaen.m-society.go.th/)
  - **Approved change:** universal (no means test), covering pregnancy from 4 months to age 6, still 600 baht/month, **from fiscal year 2027** (FY starts 1 Oct 2026). Approved by the National Child and Youth Development Committee and the Cabinet. Exact gazette wording was not seen, and some outlets give inconsistent dates — [Thansettakij](https://www.thansettakij.com/general-news/664050); [PRD Trat](https://trat.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/57/iid/344395); [The 101 World](https://www.the101.world/universal-child-support-grant-now/)
- Universal health coverage (UC/"gold card"/30-baht scheme) since 2002: population coverage **99.6%** (NHSO Annual Report 2023, as cited by UNESCAP 2026). Out-of-pocket spending is 9.9% of health spending. "30 baht treatment anywhere" went nationwide in January 2025 — [UNESCAP S1 Thailand](https://www.unescap.org/sites/default/d8files/event-documents/S1_Thailand.pdf); [Thai PBS World](https://www.thaipbsworld.com/analysis/top-health-news-stories-in-2024); [Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2376955)
- Disability allowance for children under 18: **1,000 baht/month** (800 from local government + 200 top-up). Check against the official notice — [Bangkok Biznews](https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/news/news-update/1157748)
- Birth registration: required for all children born in Thailand since the 2008 Civil Registration Act amendment. Children of unregistered or undocumented parents get a ท.ร.031 birth certificate, which does not grant Thai nationality — [Hfocus](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2018/09/16265); [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/thailand/media/5861/file)

### Inferences
- About 2.05M recipients against roughly 2.8–3.0M Thai children aged 0–6 means about 70% of eligible-age children receive the grant now (estimate: 6–7 birth cohorts of ~420–540k). It should approach 100% once the universal rule applies from FY2027.
- Game rule: a Thai-national newborn in 2026–27 gets 600 baht/month (pregnancy month 4 to age 6) plus free UC health care. Non-Thai or ท.ร.031 newborns get neither the grant nor UC. Their family pays out of pocket or uses migrant health insurance (inference from the eligibility rules).

### Gaps
- No official birth-registration completeness rate was found (MICS 2022 has it, but the figure was not in the excerpts).
- No final gazette or official DCY notice confirming the universal grant's start date and payment schedule was seen.
- Not researched: other at-birth benefits such as Social Security maternity benefit (มาตรา 33) and the child allowance for insured parents.
